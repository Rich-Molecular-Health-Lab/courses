Approved Case Source Hubs for Guided Case Conversations

Author

Dr. Alicia M. Rich

Modified

Wed Jan 21, 2026

You are encouraged (but not required) to select cases from the sources below. These sources provide real, publicly documented conservation actions with clearly stated goals, constraints, and outcomes.

You do not need to ask for approval if your case comes from one of these hubs.

Zoo & Ex Situ–Linked Conservation Programs

AZA SAFE (Saving Animals From Extinction) Species Programs
  • Species action plans and program summaries
  • Strong alignment with One Plan thinking
  • Clear goals, partners, and constraints
Zoo-Based Conservation Program Pages
  • Many accredited zoos publish conservation initiative summaries
  • Often include education, fieldwork, and captive management components

Government Recovery & Management Plans

U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service (USFWS)
  • Species recovery plans
  • Critical habitat designations
  • Reintroduction and management actions
State Wildlife Agency Conservation Programs
  • Species of Greatest Conservation Need (SGCN) plans
  • Habitat restoration or corridor projects

International & NGO Conservation Initiatives

IUCN Red List & Species Action Plans
  • Threat prioritization
  • Conservation actions and research needs
Major Conservation NGOs
  • World Wildlife Fund (WWF)
  • Wildlife Conservation Society (WCS)
  • Conservation International (CI)

Habitat Protection, Restoration & Connectivity Projects

Protected Area Creation or Expansion
  • National parks, reserves, marine protected areas
Habitat Restoration Projects
  • Wetlands, forests, coral reefs
Connectivity & Corridor Initiatives
  • Wildlife crossings
  • Landscape-scale planning

Community-Based & Human–Wildlife Conflict Projects

Community-Based Conservation Programs
  • Co-management agreements
  • Indigenous-led conservation initiatives
Human–Wildlife Conflict Mitigation
  • Compensation programs
  • Non-lethal deterrents
  • Land-use planning

Optional: Primary Literature (Use Selectively)

Primary research articles may be used if they:
  • directly inform a management decision or action
  • include explicit discussion of conservation outcomes

Primary literature is not required and should not be the sole source.
